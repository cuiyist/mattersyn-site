Solvent and a closed reaction environment

A heated closed solvent environment changes solubility, transport and precursor conversion compared with an open vessel. Changes in solvent properties and dissolution followed by reprecipitation can alter the pathways available for nucleation and crystal growth.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Hydrothermal technology for nanotechnology — DOI 10.1016/j.pcrysgrow.2007.04.001

From a metal precursor to reactive species

A metal precursor supplies the metal, while its ligands and conversion chemistry influence how reactive species become available. Stronger or more persistent coordination can slow release of reactive metal species, while ligand exchange can open alternative conversion pathways.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Mechanisms of Nucleation and Growth of Nanoparticles in Solution — DOI 10.1021/cr400544s

Ligands in solution and at surfaces

Ligands can associate with dissolved species and particle surfaces, affecting their interactions with the surrounding liquid. A ligand shell compatible with the solvent can provide a barrier to close particle approach, helping a dispersion resist aggregation.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Prospects of Colloidal Nanocrystals for Electronic and Optoelectronic Applications — DOI 10.1021/cr900137k
